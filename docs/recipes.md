# Maison de Gaby: recipe reference

Private notes for Mariam (this folder is not published on the website). Each recipe follows the best-known
version of the dessert, with quantities as the source gives them, converted to grams where possible. Methods are
short summaries; open the source for the full steps. Bake a test batch of each before selling it, and if you change
an ingredient, update the **Ingredients** text for that dessert in /admin so the allergens stay correct.

## Molten Cake: Jean-Georges Vongerichten

The New York chef credited with inventing the dish in 1987.
Source: [Cooks Without Borders, "Recipe by Jean-Georges Vongerichten"](https://cookswithoutborders.com/molten-chocolate-cake)

Makes 4 small (6 oz / 180 ml) or 3 larger ramekins.

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Unsalted butter | 113 g (1 stick), plus extra for the ramekins |
| Dark chocolate, 70% cocoa | 170 g (two 85 g bars), broken up |
| Eggs | 2 large |
| Egg yolks | 2 |
| Sugar | 50 g (¼ cup) |
| Plain flour | 2 tbsp, plus extra for the ramekins |
| Salt | a pinch |

- Butter and flour the ramekins. Melt the butter and chocolate together.
- Whisk the eggs, yolks, sugar and salt until pale and thick, then fold in the chocolate and the flour.
- Bake at **230 °C (450 °F) for 12–13 minutes**: the edges set, the centre still jiggles.
- Rest 1 minute, turn out onto a plate, and dust with icing sugar.
- For boxes: chill the unbaked batter in the ramekins, or bake just underdone and tell customers to warm for 15 seconds.

## Brownies: Baked (Brooklyn)

From Matt Lewis and Renato Poliafito's cookbook *Baked: New Frontiers in Baking*. Mixed entirely by hand so it stays
fudgy, not cakey. Sources: [Cookie Madness](https://www.cookiemadness.net/?p=4213),
[Baker by Nature](https://bakerbynature.com/?p=587)

One 23 × 33 cm (9 × 13 in) tray, cuts into 24.

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Dark chocolate, 60–72% cocoa | 310 g (11 oz), chopped |
| Unsalted butter | 227 g (1 cup) |
| Instant espresso powder | 1 tsp |
| Granulated sugar | 300 g (1½ cups) |
| Light brown sugar | 100 g (½ cup) |
| Eggs | 5 large, room temperature |
| Vanilla | 2 tsp |
| Plain flour | 160 g (1¼ cups) |
| Dark (Dutch-process) cocoa | 2 tbsp |
| Salt | 1 tsp |

- Melt the chocolate, butter and espresso over simmering water. Whisk in both sugars, then let it cool to warm.
- Whisk in the eggs a couple at a time, then the vanilla. Don't overbeat.
- Fold in the flour, cocoa and salt by hand until just combined.
- Bake in a buttered tray at **175 °C (350 °F)**. As a guide, about 30 minutes: a toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs.
- Cool completely before cutting, for clean squares.

## Carrot Cake: Jodi Elliott

The winner of Food & Wine's carrot cake taste test. Tasters picked it for being moist and tender, with restrained
cinnamon and a not-too-sweet frosting. Sources: [Food & Wine test (AOL)](https://www.aol.com/tested-4-carrot-cake-recipes-110100017.html),
[recipe reprint, credited to Food & Wine](https://www.justapinch.com/recipes/classic-carrot-cake-with-fluffy-cream-cheese.html)

Two 23 cm (9 in) layers. Your 20 cm cake will be a little taller, so check it a few minutes later.

**Cake**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pecans, toasted and chopped | 1 cup (about 115 g), plus extra to decorate |
| Plain flour | 2 cups (250 g) |
| Baking powder, baking soda, cinnamon, salt | see the source for the exact amounts |
| Vegetable or sunflower oil | 1 cup (240 ml) |
| Buttermilk | ½ cup (120 ml) |
| Vanilla | to taste (see source) |
| Eggs | 4 large |
| Sugar | 2 cups (400 g) |
| Carrots, peeled, coarsely grated | 450 g (1 lb) |

**Fluffy cream cheese frosting**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Unsalted butter, soft | 227 g (2 sticks) |
| Cream cheese, soft | 450 g (two 8 oz packs) |
| Vanilla | 1 tbsp |
| Icing sugar | 2 cups (about 240 g) |

- Whisk the dry ingredients. Separately, whisk the oil, buttermilk, vanilla, eggs and sugar. Combine, then fold in the carrots and pecans.
- Divide between two lined tins and bake until a skewer comes out clean. Cool completely.
- Beat the butter and cream cheese until fluffy, then add the vanilla and icing sugar.
- Fill, frost, and finish with toasted pecans.

## Honey Cake (Medovik): 20th Century Cafe, San Francisco

Michelle Polzine's famous ten-layer honey cake with burnt honey and dulce de leche cream. The quantities are from
Cleobuttera, a much-loved Egyptian baking blog, adapted from the New York Times version.
Sources: [Cleobuttera](https://cleobuttera.com/cakes/the-most-amazing-russian-honey-cake/),
[San Francisco Chronicle](https://preview-prod.w.sfchronicle.com/home/article/Recipe-How-to-make-20th-Century-Cafe-s-Russian-14455596.php)

One tall 23 cm (9 in) cake, about 16 slices. **Make it the day before**, because it needs a night in the fridge.

**Burnt honey**: 255 g (¾ cup) honey and 57 g (¼ cup) water.
Cook the honey to a deep amber, take it off the heat and whisk in the water. Keep ¼ cup for the cake and ½ cup for the cream.

**Cake layers**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Burnt honey | ¼ cup |
| Honey | 255 g (¾ cup) |
| Sugar | 227 g (1 cup + 2 tbsp) |
| Unsalted butter | 199 g (14 tbsp), in pieces |
| Eggs | 6 large (300 g without shells) |
| Baking soda | 2½ tsp |
| Fine salt | ¾ tsp |
| Ground cinnamon | 1 tsp |
| Plain flour | 454 g (3¾ cups) |

**Dulce de leche cream**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Burnt honey | ½ cup |
| Dulce de leche | 380 g (one can; or simmer a sealed 397 g can of sweetened condensed milk) |
| Fine salt | ½ tsp |
| Whipping cream, very cold | 1.12 L (4¾ cups) |

- Warm the honeys, sugar and butter until melted. Whisk in the eggs, then the soda, salt and cinnamon. Sift in the flour in three batches.
- Keep the batter warm over hot water so it spreads. Spread a heaping ⅓ cup onto each 23 cm circle drawn on baking paper.
- Bake each layer at **180 °C (375 °F) for 6–7 minutes**, until deep caramel and springy. That makes 11 layers.
- Toast the trimmings and the least pretty layer at 120 °C for about 15 minutes, then grind them into crumbs.
- Mix the burnt honey, dulce de leche and salt, then chill 30 minutes. Whip the cream to soft peaks, add the mixture, and whip to medium-stiff.
- Stack the 10 layers with a heaping cup of cream between each, cover the sides, and press on the crumbs.
- **Refrigerate overnight** before slicing. Baked layers freeze well, so you can bake them ahead.

## Soufflé Cheesecake: Uncle Tetsu style

Uncle Tetsu is the famous Japanese cheesecake chain. It keeps its own recipe secret, so this is the best-known close
version. Source: [Pen Online, "The Foolproof Japanese Cheesecake Recipe"](https://pen-online.com/food/the-unbeatable-japanese-cheesecake-recipe)

One 23 cm (9 in) cake.

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Milk | 130 ml |
| Cream cheese | 100 g |
| Unsalted butter | 100 g |
| Egg yolks | 8 |
| Egg whites | 13 |
| Plain flour | 60 g |
| Cornflour (cornstarch) | 60 g |
| Sugar | 130 g |
| To serve | fresh strawberries or berries, caster or icing sugar |

- Warm the milk, cream cheese and butter over low heat, whisking until smooth, then let it cool.
- Whisk in the yolks, then sift in the flour and cornflour.
- Beat the whites, adding the sugar gradually, to a thick meringue. Fold it gently into the batter.
- Line the tin with a 10 cm collar of baking paper, then set it in a tray of water (a water bath).
- Bake at **160 °C for 25 minutes, then 135 °C for 55 minutes**, until it has doubled in height.
- Dust with sugar and top with berries. A squeeze of lemon in the batter (used by some versions) brightens the flavour.
